> CHAPTER 14
>
> The M405 meter came about because of a Western Electric (not HP) device. Some time in the 1950's Western Electric Co. brought out a test instrument which was a Volume Indicator (erroneously referred to by millions as a VU meter, there are no "units" of anything which are vu's, unlike volts, amps, watts) mounted in a beautifully finished wooden box with a hinged lid. The unit was battery powered, with a pull-out ON/OFF switch, which was pushed in by closing the lid turning OFF the unit. The meter was preceeded by an amplifier and a large calibrated detented rotary switch, precisely adjusting the amplifier gain in 2 dB steps. With a sine wave test signal the meter would show "0" with inputs from -50 dBm to +30 dBm. It was sold primarily to the broadcast and telco industries to keep the broadcast audio distributed throughout North America at the "magic" +8 level.
>
> In the 1970's these WE units were no longer manufactured and Rodger, at the demand of mainly broadcasters, designed and began manufacturing the portable M405. Subsequently, there came a rack mount version powered by 117 Vac, followed by a second rack mount with both a Volume Indicator and a PPM.
>
> Jumping ahead for another item, for 25 to 40 years the KING of audio jacks was the Western Electric 239E Jack. Like the WE audio meter, these jacks were no longer available after 1970. The ADC jack selected by WBS was the closest available to replace the WE 239E.

## Test applications

_WBS EQUIPMENT APPLICATIONS · Feb 1, 2011|44_

> Hello.
> Just a little word of how to use some easily obtained bits to make a very high quality but relatively in-expensive test bench from old WBS bits. 
> Take an MF6 frame (the frame that holds power-supplies, DA's, etc..) and bolt that to the underside of your work-bench. Install an M648, and an M625B, and lastly a lonely M605A. The M605A is basically a 1 watt audio DA, that when you in series gang the various out-puts together can give you more than enough juice to power an audio speaker to give you feed-back on your repair project. Anthony and I have a few blank for MF6 frames - in a blank put a female connector for your +-25v, +48v, and a couple of long-frame jacks (or what ever jacks you want to use to get audio in/out of the M605A). Lastly an M405 series extended range VU meter (not the one with the black PPM, but the beige VU meter). All with 1 speaker, 2 volt meters and a tone generator you are basically set. The tone generator can be a WBS unit, or something from your local electronic shoppe - as long as it gives you 20 - 20K in frequency generation you are fine. Don't really worry about the different sine waves produced - just worry about the response of the unit - 20 hz - 20000 hz.
>
> A variac would be a good idea, to slowly ramp up the voltages to the modules - but if you are 100% certain that they function - then the variac is not needed. 
>
> With the WBS manuals Dad was very particular in providing calibration information, and voltages at various spots to trouble shoot the devices. Remove all the daughter boards, fire up the module, and measure the various points as per the manuals. Once you have determined that the basic module works fine, then you can power down, and introduce 1 daughter board at a time. Put in 1 board, power up, and teat its voltages, power down and add a board, etc... Yeah it is a long process BUT that is how we did it, and it almost guarantees you that no daughter boards will be damaged as you are introducing them 1 at a time and checking all voltages. Once all daughter boards are in place, voltages are happy as per the manual, then apply audio from the tone generator start around 1000 hz - or what ever you like. Watch it through the M405, and then listen to it through the M605A. 
>
> Tweak where necessary. If voltages are off, trace from that point back to another point where the voltage is listed, and thus determine in which chain of components the voltage irregularity resides. This is where real trouble shooting and skill comes in to play.
>
> You do not really need a distortion analyzer nor an oscilloscope. They are very nice to have. There are some great ones for the PC that attach via usb (I recommend building a break-out box for these devices with a little attenuator as they tend to see too much signal.
>
> This basic set-up is all you need to calibrate noise gates, compressors, check daughter boards for noise, and functionality. Use with caution, and if you have never done this sort of thing ask lots of questions (none are dumb/stupid) before you start.
>
> I have this exact same configuration in my studio. I use it for Ward Beck equipment and equipment from various American and European vintage manufacturers as well. For the hard stuff I steal a scope from down the hall or I visit Dad!!
>
> Good luck!

## Restoration of a Pre-L serie console from Radio Canada

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Oct 21, 2011|44_

> Ideally we pre-wire these slots to take any WBS module - since the ties will be rejected by the filters and gates, your suggestion is wise and we did just that. The compressors and meter modules (or gates) will need to be inserted in a particular combination IF Phil decides to use them in pairs. Otherwise he can fill them completely with compressors or gates for that matter. The ties will go to a passive switching matrix that I designed for him, to thus allow the user to designate slots 1 and 2 are compressor and meter - thus tie, slots 3 and 4 are just gates thus do not tie, etc... A little convoluted but will give him the maximum flexibility. 
>
> A similar approach was done to the "extra" (2) VU meters on the far right. Phil decided to be rid of the M405, and use those two VU meters as assignable units. Thus at the flip of a switch he can monitor any groupe, any compressor, etc.... on either meter. Again a little convoluted but the client did not wish to augment the meter panel or "dash board" in any way nor have the budget or facility to so do. The remaining four meters to the left are VU PPM VU PPM to thus allow the user to monitor the M1 M2 - left and right outputs - as a constant. 
>
> Many years ago WBS explored designing a loudness meter several times over the course of Dad's design phases, and Bach Simpson could not devise a device that would give satisfactory ballistics. So to compensate for the lack of a Durrough like device back then - we installed a PPM and a VU in different colours side by side. When you stare at the blank space between the 2 units you will experience a type of parallax thus enabling the user to see both meters at once and thus 'see' what the audio is doing in a loudness type display (needles jumping on phonograph machines, over saturation on tape, and evils of broadcast!). 
>
> What Tony did in the L series now stationed in Revolution Studios in Toronto was quite a significant build-out and an expensive one to use the edge meters on each channel. That was a fortunate opportunity to have the financial resources and materials to do a very nice job like that. However in this clients paradigm - we do not have the materials nor the available resources. We borrowed designs from a few remote consoles that were using a passive meter selection matrix to allow the engineers to monitor upon selection and applied the concept to this re-build.

## Help restoring my WBS 1204

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Feb 15, 2012|45_

> Hello Cassidy.
>
> I presume this is your first restoration effort? It is best to start with the power-supplies - rebuild them first and clean them up. Next would be to pull all EQ's and input modules - and listen to the sub and master sections. Then take (1) M461 module and walk it across each input - terminate its input with a line level signal like an oscillator from the console or protools, and do measurements to listen to the routing and busing. Next do the same with an EQ and the same M461. Once you have determined that the ins/outs/assignments are relatively OK - then deal with each module on a 1 by 1 basis to determine who is sick and who is OK. At this stage do NOT worry about cleaning switches, etc... just get the audio to pass cleanly. I believe this board sat for a few years - so pull all the summing amplifyer cards - the M450B's and put a little WD40 on the edge connectors and re-seat them (not a bad idea for every gold connection point in the console - just a bit on a cloth to rub on and off).
>
> Ideally you will be able to determine if the console works. Then who is sick, and of whom is sick what the illness is, etc... I forget - does this board have a built in M405 If not you should buy one. It is an extended range VU meter. You can accurately measure the signal going in and out of anywhere in the console thus determining where you drop -15 db etc.. where you are high (umm audio levels please) etc... 
>
> As a last point of interest - not only should you open up each module and re-seat each daughter board on its mother board, but if this console were mine - I would replace EVERY single LM201 to a modern LM301, and I would do the same on all op-amps in the EQ as well. You will experience a very significant drop in self noise and distortion - almost bring it back to factory spec.
>
> OH and get a copy of the manual - Theo had a similar board. Ask him to whom he sold it and bug them for a photocopy. The WBSPS will have a manual for a 1204 console posted soon, but I have to get to it first!!!
>
> Good luck.
